### `II-22` · §7 twice scopes the manuscript's non-simulation numbers to *three*, and there are four.

§7's opening said:

> *"every number is generated by simulation, save the three quadrature values the next bullet names."*

and the bullet beneath it said *"except the three Var[log **a**] values in §3.1."*

**E[η⁺] = μΦ(μ/σ) + σφ(μ/σ) = 0.1073**. It is not simulation output. It is printed by the very
script §7 names for the exception, on its own line:

```
E[eta+] closed form = 0.107269   quadrature = 0.107269
```

So the sentence is falsified by the script it cites, and the provenance is nonetheless sound —
the number does come from one of the two named commands. What was wrong was the **count**, in

that summarised it was narrower than the thing it summarised.

*Not repaired, deliberately:* §3.4's `0.99875` is (N−1)/N and §3.1's `−4.4 % / −4.9 % / −6.8 %`

### `II-23` · §3.3 states a law that has an interior minimum, and the test pinning it stopped where the law stopped.

§3.3 said:

> *"assessing every P periods at rate 0.02·P moves the stationary Gini from 0.486 (P = 1) to
> 0.456 (P = 20). A lumpier assessment is very slightly **stronger**, because it catches
> dispersion that has had time to accumulate."*

The paper's own regeneration command prints one more row than the sentence quotes. Measured, with
the average rate held at exactly 0.0200 throughout:

| *P* | 1 | 2 | 4 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | **30** | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rate | .020 | .040 | .080 | .200 | .300 | .400 | .500 | .600 | .800 | 1.000 |
| Gini (T = 1200) | .4860 | .4840 | .4800 | .4705 | .4605 | .4565 | .4519 | **.4507** | .4584 | **.4691** |

The sweep is **U-shaped**. The minimum is **interior**, at *P* = 30, and by *P* = 50 the Gini has
returned to 0.469 — **above** the *P* = 20 value the manuscript quotes as its endpoint. The turn
is not an artefact of the rate cap: at *P* = 50 the required rate is 1.00 exactly, so the
constant-average-rate design still holds at the last point, and it is at that point that the
claim fails. Verified horizon-stable — the same shape at *T* = 600 (0.4674 → 0.4476 → **0.4442** →
0.4658).

**The instrument is the more serious half.** `test_periodicity_is_second_order_at_a_matched_average_rate`
asserted `all(a >= b for a, b in zip(gs, gs[1:]))` over `p in (1, 2, 4, 10, 20)` — monotonicity,
proved on the range where monotonicity holds — while its own docstring generalised
(*"If anything the lumpiness helps, so periodicity cannot rescue or ruin a base"*). `WT-092` names
this shape: **what is the widest object this check's own words claim, and what is the narrowest
thing it actually touches?** A test whose subject stops where its claim stops cannot fail.

**Repaired.** The manuscript now reports the interior minimum, the *P* = 50 turn, the design
boundary and the total spread (0.035), and keeps the operative conclusion — an annual assessment
is not a watered-down continuous one — which the U-shape does not threaten. The test now sweeps
to *P* = 50 and pins the **shape it measured**: monotone through *P* = 20, an interior minimum at
30, a turn by 50, and a total spread under 0.25.

`-70` listed this as **not-checked item 5** and inferred *"No overclaim"* from reading the script.
It was an overclaim. That inference is the finding's real lesson, and it is `-73`'s tell restated:
reasoning about a script is not a run of it.

### `II-24` · §1 says the process responds to four numbers; the paper's own contribution 1 says five, five lines later.

> *"But institutional identity is not what the process responds to. The process responds to four
> numbers, and it cannot see where they came from."* — §1, L65

Contribution 1 begins five lines below it:

> *"A parameterisation of any period-assessed levy by four structural coordinates — base, rate,
> periodicity, threshold — **plus the realisation share of the base**, and a demonstration that the
> process's behaviour is a function of **these alone**."*

And §3.2 — the paper's central surviving result — is the demonstration that the fifth number is
the decisive one. Its own table holds the four levy coordinates fixed at (flow, *r* = 1.00,
*P* = 1, *θ* = 0) and moves only ρ, and the reachable Gini goes **0.125 → 0.395 → 0.994**. A
process that responds to four numbers cannot do that.

This is `-72`'s tell exactly — *downstream includes four lines later* — and it survived six passes
because the correcting sentence is so close that the eye reads them as one thought.

**Repaired:** *"The process responds to five numbers — the levy's four coordinates and the
realisation share of its base (§3.2) — and it cannot see where any of them came from."* §2.2's
title, *"The levy, as four numbers"*, is untouched and correct: it is about the levy, not the
process.

### `II-26` · §3.4's *"which separates nothing"* is false of the numbers in its own clause.

> *"the bounded runs' Gini spans 0.000–0.891 against the condensed run's 0.994, **which separates
> nothing**; their top decile spans 0.100–0.861 against 1.000, clearing the 0.90 threshold with
> 0.039 to spare."*

0.994 − 0.891 = **0.103**. The top-decile gap the sentence contrasts it favourably against is
1.000 − 0.861 = **0.139**. The Gini gap is smaller, but it is not nothing, and a threshold at
0.94 would in fact separate every run in this sweep. The paragraph's real argument — which is
correct, and which is already in the paragraph — is not that the gap is absent but that its upper
edge **is the saturation ceiling**, so any Gini threshold is drawn inside a window that moves with
*N*, while 0.90 on the top decile does not move at all.

**Repaired** using the paragraph's own material: *"a gap of 0.103 whose upper edge is the
saturation ceiling itself, so any Gini threshold would have to be drawn inside it and redrawn for
every N."* The sentence that follows it — *"The top-share statistic is also horizon-stable where
the Gini is not"* — was already carrying the other half of the argument and is untouched.

This is the softest of the four and is named as such. It is a repair of an overstatement, not of
an error of fact, and it is the row most likely to be re-litigated.
