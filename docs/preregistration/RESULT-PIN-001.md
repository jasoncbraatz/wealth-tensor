## 1 · The find

Paper III **§11** (line 1996) tells a replicator where the code is: *"**Code state for the results reported here:** commit **d655501** (last commit touching `src/`) … **d655501** is the SHA a replicator needs and is verifiable now."* The parenthetical is the whole of the assurance — it is what licenses a single global SHA to stand for the state of every module the paper uses — and it is false. `git log -1 --format=%h -- src/` returns `93a159b`. Four commits have touched `src/` since `d655501` (2026-08-05): `6492157` and `623a117` on 2026-08-10, `0a91001` on 2026-08-11 adding `recognition_fold.py`, and `93a159b` on 2026-08-13 adding twenty-one lines to `src/wealth_tensor/edgar.py`. The charitable reading — *last commit touching the modules this paper uses* — fails on the same evidence, because `edgar.py` is one of the three modules §11's own **Modules** bullet names and `93a159b` is one day older than this paper's head. The sentence invites the check that falsifies it, and the check costs one command.

The same section carries the same defect a second time, nineteen lines earlier: *"**100 tests at the pinned commit d655501** … The head of the repository carries 103. The three later additions guard claims this paper makes and change no model code."* The head carries **777**. There are 677 later additions. The three that are named are still correctly named; the count they are subtracted from is not.

**Neither sentence was wrong when it was written, and that is what distinguishes this costume from the five before it.** `-23`–`-25` found a mis-scoped number, `-26` adjectives promoted past their measurement, `-27` adjectives flagged load-bearing and never built, `-33` a scope restriction read against a sample selection. Each of those was an error at the moment of typing. This one was a true sentence that the programme's own subsequent correct work falsified — including `93a159b`, the commit that repaired the REG-006 tag defect. `LEDGER` WT-052 records `d655501` as *"both the PRE-002 commit and the last `src/` commit"* on 2026-08-10, the same day two commits stopped that being true. The tell that catches it is not *is this quantity measured?* but **does this sentence's truth value depend on something that can change without anyone editing the sentence?**

The instrument-vs-prose split is why five sessions of grepping walked past it. `d655501` occurs six times in this repository and every one of them is prose — paper III four times, `LEDGER.md`, `RESULT-002-wt026.md`, the session notes. It occurs zero times in `scripts/`, `tests/` or `src/`. Every other load-bearing SHA in this estate — `93a159b`, `b02d02e`, `656b914`, `6a5094a`, `e216037` — sits in an instrument's header, next to code that would notice. The one SHA a replicator most needs is the one nothing guards.

## 2 · The repair, registered before it was performed

**Registered before the edit:** the claim is right but under-armed, so the repair is a **steelman and not a cut**. What §11 is trying to guarantee is that the code which produced §5, §2 and §A.2 has not moved; a *global* SHA is simply the wrong instrument for a *per-file* guarantee, and it fails the moment any module in the repository changes for any reason. The per-file pins are strictly stronger: they survive every future commit that does not touch the pinned files, they name `edgar.py` at `d655501`, `lag.py` at `ad779eb` (its only commit) and `lambda_sensitivity.py` at `b9089c7`, and they let §11 **disclose** the REG-006 correction rather than leave it hidden behind a stale global pin. The disclosure is checkable in the strongest available form: `93a159b` appends the corrected tier-0 tag list beside the registered one without editing it, so the `TIER_TAGS` block that selected §5's published sample is **byte-identical at `d655501` and at the head of the repository** (sha256 `e7fce6a9aae7b94ecf8b7a346be50a8f06c7095a97b6e0e5fc885a2f233b0827`). The test-suite sentence is repaired by stating the growth positively — the suite is larger at head and grows with every registration — and keeping the three named additions, which are still correctly described. **Two edits, both inside §11, and no other section is touched.** No claim is weakened, no hedge is added, no free parameter is introduced, and nothing is absorbed into the manuscript as a caveat. G-COACH-3 is evaluated across the edit with `defensive_count.py --against` on the pre-edit file, and the full suite is run after it.

**And the repair is registered together with its guard, because the defect's signature is that nobody edited the sentence.** `tests/test_pin001_code_state.py` asserts that each pinned path's latest commit is the SHA §11 discloses for it, so the next edit to a pinned module goes red and the pin and the manuscript must move in the same commit — the committed-baseline pattern G-COACH-3 already uses. It asserts the `TIER_TAGS` byte-identity the disclosure claims, which checks the substance and not merely the SHA. It asserts that the two rotted phrases are gone. It **imports** every SHA from the edit script rather than retyping any of them, so the subject is written down once and the guard cannot drift from its own witness, and it skips rather than fails outside a git work tree. It will be proved by **mutation** before it is trusted.

**What this does not do.** It does not pin a head-of-repository SHA — that is still deferred to posting, as §11 already says, and the per-file pins are what replaces it in the meantime. It does not claim `93a159b` left §5's numbers unchanged in general; it claims the narrower and checkable thing, that the registered `TIER_TAGS` block is byte-identical, which is the fact §5's published counts depend on. It does not touch `REVIEW-004`'s separate and still-unrepaired finding that paper III says *"refused five times"* three times over a list of four — that is a different registration and is carded separately. And it does not repair §4's coverage silence or §7.5's two errata, which stay recorded by standing ruling.
