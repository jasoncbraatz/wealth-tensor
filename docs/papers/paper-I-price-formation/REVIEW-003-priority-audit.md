# REVIEW-003 · Priority audit on Paper I's surviving claim · **DISPLACED — Wicksteed (1910)**

- **Audited:** the one claim `REVIEW-002` left standing after four FATAL findings — that supply and
  demand are not independent *as functions of the allocation*, the allocation cancelling from their
  difference identically, *z*(*p*) = #{*mᵢ* > *p*} − *S*, at every non-reservation price.
- **Method:** three agents. One hunting priority. One told that **an over-eager priority claim is as
  damaging as a missed one** (L28). One instructed to **REFUTE the displacement** — that is, to
  defend this paper.
- **Verdict: DISPLACES.** Philip H. Wicksteed, *The Common Sense of Political Economy* (Macmillan,
  1910), **Book II Ch. IV, "Buyer and Seller. Demand and Supply."** Same claim, same horse market,
  same reallocation exercise.
- **Every quote below was verified first-hand against the 1910 Macmillan first edition**, not
  accepted from an agent. The Econlib/OLL text states its edition in its front matter and was
  checked.

## 1 · The defence attorney found the murder weapon

The agent instructed to *refute* the displacement returned it **stronger** than the prosecution
had. It caught that the first auditor's own quotation had ellipsis-ed out the single most damaging
sentence in the book:

> "Now this curve is a curve of reserve prices, which … is merely another name for the demand curve
> of those who possess a stock of the commodity … **The connection between these two pieces of
> information is arbitrary; for the vital information as to excess of supply over that required to
> bring the ordinates to a certain point, might just as well have been given us in connection with
> the other (so-called "demand") curve, or partly in connection with one and partly in connection
> with the other, or without any specified connection with either of them.** Thus, if we had not had
> the two curves given us at all, but only the whole collective curve, **without distinction between
> possessor and non-possessor** … we should have obtained exactly the same result."

That is the identity, in words, in 1910: the quantity belongs to *neither* curve and may be attached
to either or to neither.

## 2 · The three escape hatches, each tested and each closed

**"He only claims invariance at equilibrium."** No. He computes stock-minus-collective-demand at
explicitly non-equilibrium prices, twice, and says the reference point is free: *"We could of course
have started at any other point of (b) lower than 40, and the corresponding point of (a), with the
same result."* And on Marshall's corn market: *"the excess over the amount required to bring them
all to the point 37s. remained 400."* At 37s. Marshall's own numbers give supply 1000, demand 600,
excess 400 — off equilibrium, re-derived from the pooled curve.

**"He varies the stock, not the price."** True of one passage and false of the chapter. Fig. 27 is
every-*S* at a fixed reading; Figs. 22–25 and the Marshall table are every-*p* at fixed *S*.

**"He does not do the unit-demand indivisible case."** He does. It is Hobson's horse market —
eight sellers £10–£26, ten buyers £15–£30 — and **Fig. 27 is literally #{*mᵢ* > *p*}**: one
descending step function over eighteen agents with no possessor/non-possessor distinction. Figs. 28
and 29 are two *different* arbitrary partitions of those same eighteen, and he says both give the
same answer, and that any other would too:

> "**The irrelevant facts are that the eight horses are at present in the possession of A-H**, and
> that I-R are all without horses."

> "Given, then, the relevant facts, **you may distribute the items between the groups just as you
> like** … you will always bring out the identical result."

He also anticipates our tie caveat (*"It may be less than a farthing"*) and states our
no-endowment-effect assumption and its limits.

## 3 · He owns our §5 thesis too, and states our corollary more precisely than we do

> "**The method of intersection is, in fact, a mere disguise of the method of addition**, and it
> might ignore the distinction between buyer and seller without affecting the result … if adopted to
> shew the ultimate considerations that determine the market price, it is, to say the least of it,
> **seriously misleading and mischievous**."

Paper I §5 in one sentence, 1910, better argued.

**And our corollary is overclaimed.** We wrote that the supply/demand split *"carries no economic
content."* Wicksteed says it has content for exactly one thing —

> "the initial distribution of the stock **affects the amount of business done** and the movements
> that bring about equilibrium; but it does not affect the price or the ultimate distribution"

— and none for price and final allocation. He is right and we were sloppy. **Volume traded is
allocation-dependent; excess demand is not.** That distinction must appear in any re-scope.

## 4 · Where the paper is a special case, and where it is not

**Within the market, ours is the special case and this must be conceded in print.** Our identity
needs each holder to hold exactly one unit. Wicksteed's damson stall-keeper (28 lb reserved at 4d,
21 lb at 5d, none at 6d) and Marshall's corn market are **divisible goods with multi-unit
holdings**, and get the same result. He does not need our restriction.

**Böhm-Bawerk is the other way round, and Jason's reading is correct there.** *Positive Theory of
Capital* (Smart trans.), p. 203 — ten buyers at £30/28/26/24/22/21/20/18/17/15, eight sellers at
£10/11/15/17/20/21:10s/25/26 — and p. 209:

> "If, finally, we substitute the short and significant name of **'Marginal Pairs'** … The market
> price is limited and determined by the subjective valuations of the two Marginal Pairs."

His statement needs **four parties in two pairs**, *"in every case it is the narrower limit that
decides."* Pool the eighteen and set *S* = 8: 8th-highest 21.5, 9th 21 — the bracket in one line,
two order statistics, the buyer/seller labels surplus. **That is not us being his special case; it
is the distinction his statement requires being dissolved.** Arithmetic re-verified against Jason's
own copy, not taken from `REVIEW-002`.

**The lineage is therefore: Böhm-Bawerk (1889) states the two-sided form → Wicksteed (1910) shows
the two-sidedness is irrelevant → Paper I restated Wicksteed.**

## 5 · What is left, stated at the strength the audit supports

Two things, and neither is a paper:

- the jump structure of *z* **at** reservation prices, which Wicksteed handles verbally;
- that gross volume min(*D*, *S*) is allocation-dependent while *z* is not — he **asserts** it and
  never derives it.

**"We wrote it in symbols" is not a contribution** and is not offered as one.

## 6 · Disposition

**Paper I is re-scoped around P3 · Atomism** (Jason, 2026-08-11) — excess demand is a fold over
units; the two schedules are folds over units *and the allocation*, which is not a property of the
population. Wicksteed has no P3; his apparatus is subjective scales of preference and is
domain-bound by construction. Paper I was not scooped so much as **written one level too low**: it
went out as a market result and met the man who owns markets.

**That re-scope is currently unsupported.** The test built to exercise it —
`REG-001` / `WT-066` — **returned no verdict**, and its own failure report sits beside this one.
Paper I's limitations section must say the generality is unexercised, in those words.

**Citations now mandatory, whatever the paper becomes:** Wicksteed (1910) Bk II Ch. IV **in front**,
Böhm-Bawerk (1889, Smart trans.) for *marginal pairs*, Shapley & Shubik (1971) whose §4 is titled
*"The Horse Market of Böhm-Bawerk"*, Theocharis (1960), Fisher (1961) and Bischi et al. (2010)
eq. 2.26 for §4.2, and Coase (1960) and Gorman (1953) as adjacent-not-displacing.

**On obscurity, recorded because it is a live temptation.** Wicksteed and Böhm-Bawerk are not
mainstream and most working economists could not summarise either. That is a reason to cite them
*harder*, not a licence to lean on it. Recovering a result the profession dropped is an honest
contribution; standing on it hoping nobody checks is not. The Palgrave has an entry —
Steedman, *"Reservation Price and Reservation Demand"*, whose bibliography is **two items, both
Wicksteed** — so referees who know this exist.
