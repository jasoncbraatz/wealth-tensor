## 0 · Read this first, because it changes what the document is for

**THE CORPUS CARRIES ZERO FIGURE CAPTIONS AND TWENTY-THREE TABLES.** Measured, not asserted: a
count of markdown header-separator rows across the three in-scope manuscripts returns 3 in
paper-II, 19 in paper-III and 1 in paper-IV, over 181 table lines. So this is **not** a document
about where the existing charts go. There are no charts. It is a document about which of these
tables is a picture that has been printed as a grid of numbers.

**A SESSION MAY PROPOSE CHART FORMS AND MUST NOT BUILD THEM (§ 4.3). Nothing here is drawn.**
Every row below is a proposal with its reasoning attached, and every one of them is refusable.
The three columns most worth arguing with are **which claim it carries**, **must follow**, and
**chart candidate** — the first two are checkable against the manuscript, and the third is taste.

**The `which claim it carries` column is doing double duty as a defect check.** § 4.3: *"the
specific sentence that fails without it — **if none, it is a C-c orphan, not a figure**."* Every
one of the twenty-three tables was put to that test and **every one carries a sentence.** There
are no orphans. That is consistent with Pass A's census (C-c = 1 corpus-wide, repaired by Pass C)
and it is the second time the corpus has passed this particular check.

### 0.1 · The four things worth knowing before reading the table

**ONE. Three tables are the highest-value conversions in the corpus, and they are not the
biggest ones.** `III-T19` (§ A.2.4's sawtooth), `III-T4 + III-T5` as a pair (the inversion beside
the statistic it spares), and `IV-T1` (the whitespace scale). Each is a claim about a *shape*
currently asserted with summary statistics, which is the worst available way to assert a shape.

**TWO. The single largest table should not be drawn at all.** § 7's survivals ledger is ~50 rows
× 4 columns with a full sentence per row. Its value is per-row auditability, and its problem is
**pagination, not visualisation**. See § 3.

**THREE. Six proposed figures correspond to no table.** The mechanism at paper-III § 2, the root
exchange at § 4.2, the scale continuum at § 4.2, the inversion pair, paper-II § 3.1's nested
frontiers, and paper-IV § 5's crossing height. **Two of those carry a paper's title claim and have
neither a table nor a figure today** — `NEW-F3` (paper-III's sharpest statement) and `NEW-F5`
(paper-II's title, carried by one sentence). If only two things get drawn, draw those.

**FOUR. Several proposals need a series this document has not verified exists.** Where a chart
needs the *full* sweep behind a sampled table, the row says so; the fallback in every such case is
`TABLE — stays a table`, never a bar chart of the sample. The commands that would produce them are
in each manuscript's data-and-code section.

### 0.2 · What the ids mean

`II-T2` is paper-II's second table, in document order. `NEW-F1` is a proposed figure with no
current table. The ids are stable and are meant to be cited in layout without re-deriving which
table is which.

entire value is per-row auditability — a reader arrives to check one claim, not to absorb a trend.
Any chart would have to project fifty heterogeneous outcomes (relative errors, percentages,
Kendall τ, z-statistics, event counts, a dollar example) onto an axis they do not share, and would
discard the killing-condition column, which is the only thing separating this from a list of
successes. **The problem is pagination, not visualisation.**

**III-T17 and III-T18 · § A.2.3, the η invariance pair.** One is a column of **exact zeros**; the
other is a slope of **1.000000000000**. Both claims live in decimal places. Drawn, the first
becomes a flat line the reader must be *told* is exactly flat, and the second a line whose slope no
reader resolves past the second decimal. Strictly less informative than the printed number.

**II-T1 · § 2.2, the four coordinates.** A definitional glossary with no relationship among its
rows to display — and the obvious visual treatment would silently assert § 3.3's *finding* in the
furniture of a definition. **Encoding a result in the layout of a definition is how a paper starts
assuming its own conclusion.**

**III-T9 · § 5.1, the predicted ordering.** Four labels and an arrow. It should not be drawn; it
should be **absorbed**, as the consistently-ordered shared x-axis of III-T4, III-T5, III-T10,
III-T11 and the censoring chart.

**III-T2 · § 3.1, the entropy-rate sweep.** Three points whose load-bearing content is the prose
*sketch* column. A chart keeps 0/16/100 and loses the labels, and the 100 swamps the row that
matters most — the zero.

**III-T13 · § 5.3, the negative control — *only if the permutation draws are unavailable.*** Drawn
from the four-number summary alone it becomes a bar of a mean with an error bar: a distribution
with its distribution removed. **Draw the histogram or leave the table alone; do not draw the
summary.** The draws are in `RESULT-002-*-run.log` — check before defaulting.

**And a general caution for the percentage tables — III-T6, III-T8, IV-T1.** All three invite bar
charts and none of them wants one. III-T6's claim is about a curve's shape *between* its rows;
III-T8's is about three lines converging then diverging; IV-T1's is about *position on a calibrated
scale*. **In each case the bar chart preserves the numbers and destroys the claim.**

## 4 · The two rulings this document makes, so they can be overruled

**RULING 1 · Nothing here is drawn, including the six proposals that would be trivial to draw.**
§ 4.3 is explicit — *"the plan is the deliverable; the drawing is his"* — and the reason holds
independently: a chart drawn by the session that proposed it arrives with its own defence attached,
and the author would be editing a picture rather than choosing one.

**RULING 2 · Where a chart needs a series this document has not confirmed exists, the fallback is
the table and never a bar chart of the sampled rows.** Six proposals depend on a sweep behind a
sampled table (II-T2, II-T3, III-T2's alternative, III-T13, III-T19, NEW-F1). Each says so at the
site. Bar-charting the samples instead would convert a claim about a curve into a claim about five
points, which is a different and weaker claim wearing the same ink.
